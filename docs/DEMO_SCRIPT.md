# Personal demo · 3 minutes + 30 seconds
## 0:00–0:30
This is First-Proof, my Week 9 Operator slice. It coordinates a bounded paid micro-experience only when existing work cannot fill a specific evidence gap. This is a fictional prototype: no real payments, hiring decisions or verified credentials.
## 0:30–1:00
Here is Alex's prior academic work and the gap: noticing missing conditions before recommending a supplier. The business funds the task, Alex pays nothing, and the scope is fixed at four hours. We identify the reviewer, criteria and help channel before starting.
## 1:00–1:45
I start the task, then simulate the supervisor becoming unavailable. The task pauses automatically and submission is blocked. I assign a substitute, but that does not automatically restart work. If funding is also missing, resume stays blocked. Once every condition is restored, I explicitly resume.
## 1:45–2:20
Repeated similar work triggers a separate review. If these assignments resemble a continuing junior role, the coordinator stops the micro-experience and moves toward a formal pathway. Replacing the supervisor cannot bypass that alert.
## 2:20–3:00
Evidence stays narrow. AI drafts a simulated checklist and cannot upgrade status. A human review and walkthrough are separate simulated steps. Employer opening and action are tracked separately, as is payment actually received. Mechanical testing found that the engine allowed review while paused; I fixed it and repeated the tests before redeployment.
## 3:00–3:30 · What changed my mind
I began by thinking the opportunity itself was the main product. The Blueprint changed my mind: existing experience comes first, and new work needs a specific reason. Building also showed me that an unavailable supervisor is not a minor inconvenience: it breaks the evidence and the learning. The product must protect the handoff, including the right to pause or stop, rather than just count completed tasks.
